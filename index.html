Why the numbers are shown

A calculator is only useful if you can check it. When a loan calculator tells you the payment is $386.66 and nothing else, you have no way to know whether it used your rate as annual or monthly, whether it compounded correctly, or whether it quietly rounded the term. So every tool here prints the inputs it actually used alongside the result, and states the formula in plain form underneath.

That matters most for the money tools. A rate entered as 6 instead of 0.06, or a term entered in years where the tool expected months, changes the answer by an order of magnitude — and those mistakes are invisible when only the final figure is displayed.

Nothing here is saved or sent anywhere. Every calculation runs in your browser, so the numbers you type never leave your device.
